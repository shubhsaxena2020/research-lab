## Verbatim evidence

**1. Resilient Semantic Threat Detection at the Edge** — https://doi.org/10.55041/ijcope.v2i8.090
> "By fine-tuning this architecture on the UCI SMS Spam Collection, we achieved a testing accuracy of 99.04% and a weighted F1-score of 0.9904."

> "Notably, the model maintained a precision of 0.97 on the minority spam class"

Both quoted from the OpenAlex abstract; the DOI landing page (fetch 1 of 3, 2026-11-03) reproduces the same abstract and shows no threshold/prevalence table. The paper's headline selection criterion is weighted F1 — the exact practice the C3 slice argues against — and the imbalance claim is made without reporting prevalence or alert volume.

**2. A TRANSFORMER-BASED DISTILBERT FRAMEWORK FOR REAL-TIME DETECTION OF PHISHING ATTACKS IN SMS AND WHATSAPP** — https://doi.org/10.58257/ijprems51068
> "Experimental results show that the proposed model achieves an accuracy of 99.28%, a precision of 97.96%, a recall of 96.64%, and an F1score of 97.30%"

("F1score" is verbatim in the source abstract.) Metrics are listed as standard classification metrics at one implicit threshold; no base rate, no alerts per N, no cost ratio, no Indian-English data.

## Summary

- No COLLISION in this batch: neither paper publishes an operating-point table at sub-1% prevalence with a cost-ratio threshold.
- Both are CLOSE on the weaker axis (SMS detection at imbalanced-but-not-sub-1% rates, single F1-selected operating point on UCI SMS Spam).
- Novelty space for C3 remains open in this batch; the slice's distinctive elements (alert volume, cost ratio, 0.1–1% prevalence, code-mixed Indian English) are untouched by both.
