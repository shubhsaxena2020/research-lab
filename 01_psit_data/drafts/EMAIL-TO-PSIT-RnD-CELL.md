# Email to the PSIT R&D cell, and questions for a faculty mentor

**What this page is for.** Two things Shubh can use this week: an email that asks PSIT's Research & Innovation cell for the facts public sources cannot give, and a short list of questions for a first meeting with a faculty mentor. Nothing here has been sent. The recipient address is deliberately a placeholder, because no R&D cell address is published on the institute website (see the checklist at the end).

## 1. Subject line options

Pick one. The first is the safest for an institutional mailbox.

1. Request for PSIT research information: publications, patents and funded projects
2. First-year BCA student: request for research data and a short meeting
3. Public-data study of PSIT's research output: request for institutional details

## 2. The email

Copy the block below. Replace `[RECIPIENT EMAIL — TO BE CONFIRMED]` with the address you verify, and fill the two bracketed lines in the signature. The body is 240 words, counted without the To and Subject lines.

```text
To: [RECIPIENT EMAIL — TO BE CONFIRMED]
Subject: Request for PSIT research information: publications, patents and funded projects

Dear Sir / Madam,

I am Shubh Saxena, a first-semester BCA student at PSIT College of Higher Education, Kanpur.

For a personal study, I am compiling a picture of PSIT's research output from public sources: Crossref, Europe PMC, PubMed and the institute website. I have 793 publication records so far, dated 2007 to 2026. Public sources do not carry the details below, and I would be grateful for your help:

1. Publications by PSIT faculty for the last three academic years, with department.
2. Patents filed and granted, with year and status.
3. Funded projects, with agency, amount and duration.
4. Whether the institute has a student research policy.
5. Whether faculty mentor undergraduate projects, and how a student can approach them.
6. Whether there is an internal research seminar, journal club or society that a first-year student can join.

I will share the compiled dataset and a short summary with your office once it is checked, in case it is useful for IQAC or NAAC work.

I am writing to your office because the Research and Innovation cell covers the whole institute. If the College of Higher Education has a separate research coordinator, please direct me there.

May I meet you for fifteen minutes, or would you prefer to reply by email? I can come at any time.

Thank you.

Yours sincerely,
Shubh Saxena
First-semester BCA, PSIT College of Higher Education, Kanpur
[your mobile number] | [your email address]
```

Two notes on wording. "Personal study" is deliberate: do not describe this as institute work or as a project sanctioned by anyone, because it is not. The offer to share the dataset back is what makes the request worth answering; keep it even if the reply is short.

## 3. If they ask why

Use this if the reply is "why do you want this?" or if you are asked in person.

> I am trying to understand what research at PSIT actually looks like before I ask anyone to supervise me. The public databases show the papers but not the patents, the projects or the policies, and those are the parts that decide whether a first-year student can do anything real here. I am not asking for anything confidential: publications, patents and funded projects are the same categories the institute already reports to NAAC and NIRF. If the data is not mine to see, I would rather be told so than guess, and I will say in my write-up that the institute did not provide it.

## 4. Questions for a faculty mentor (15 minutes)

Ask these in order. They are chosen so that each one has a short answer, and so that the last two give you the next person to talk to.

1. Which faculty here work on machine learning or data science, and may I see two or three of their recent papers?
2. Does the institute have a GPU or a lab machine that students can use for model training, and what is the process to get access?
3. Do faculty supervise undergraduate project work outside the curriculum, and at what stage can a first-year student start?
4. What would be a realistic first project for me, given where I am in the first semester: some programming, no research experience?
5. Would you be willing to supervise or co-author a small study if I do the data work and the writing?
6. What does the institute expect from a student who puts its name on a paper: prior approval, an acknowledgement, an IP check?
7. Is there any internal seed funding, travel grant or conference support for student work?
8. Whom else should I speak to, and may I use your name when I write to them?

Do not ask all eight if the conversation is going well; ask 1, 3, 4 and 5 first, since they decide whether there is anything to do at all.

## 5. Do not send this yet: checklist

- [ ] **Confirm the real address.** No R&D cell email is published on the institute site as of 2026-11-03. The Contact page lists only `admission@psit.ac.in` and phone numbers [1]; the NAAC Self Study Report gives `director@psit.ac.in` and an IQAC coordinator address [2]; the research policy circular carries `info@psit.ac.in` on its letterhead [6]. Verify by calling the toll-free number 7670998888 and asking for the office of the Dean, Research & Innovation, or by asking your own department office. **Do not send to a guessed address** such as research@psit.ac.in; a bounce teaches you nothing and looks careless.
- [ ] **Decide which campus you are writing to.** The Dean, Research & Innovation sits at PSIT (engineering, NH-19 campus) [3][4]. You study at PSIT College of Higher Education, which is a separate entity with its own college code [1][5]. Send to PSIT's R&D office and ask to be redirected if CHE has its own coordinator; the email already says this.
- [ ] **Read `00_foundations/WHAT-A-PAPER-DOES-FOR-YOU.md` first.** It explains what a publication is worth to you, which is the thing you are actually asking for. Without it you are asking for data you cannot yet use.
- [ ] **Fill the signature.** Your mobile number and your own email address, not the college's.
- [ ] **Attach nothing in the first email.** Offer the dataset instead; an unsolicited attachment from an unknown sender is usually filtered.
- [ ] **Log what you sent.** Date, address, and a copy, in `sessions/S1-foundations/STATUS.md`, so the next session knows the request is pending.
- [ ] **Set a follow-up date.** One short reminder after ten working days, then ask a faculty mentor in person. Do not send a third email.

## How confident is this?

- **Solid.** The institute's public contact details, the name of the Dean, Research & Innovation, and the fact that the R&I cell exists and covers the institute. All four are on pages that load today.
- **Solid.** That no R&D cell email address is published: the Contact page carries only the admissions address and phone numbers, the SSR carries the Director's and IQAC addresses, and the policy circular carries the general institute address.
- **UNCERTAIN.** Whether a reply will come, and how fast. A first-semester student asking an administrative office for data is a low-priority request; the follow-up rule exists for that reason.
- **UNCERTAIN.** Which office owns the College of Higher Education's research records. The CHE site is thin, and the split between the two entities is exactly the ambiguity described in `01_psit_data/STATUS-OF-RESEARCH.md` section 4.
- **Not covered.** Any scholarship, fee or eligibility question; this email is only about research information.
