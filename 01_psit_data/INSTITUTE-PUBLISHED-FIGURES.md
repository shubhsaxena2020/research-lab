Nothing here is estimated. Where a figure exists only as a sentence in a PDF, it is labelled as such. All sources retrieved 2026-11-03.

## 3. How each figure is defined (in the institute's own words)

- **"Research papers" in the SSR means UGC-CARE journal papers only.** Metric 3.3.1 counts "research papers in the Journals notified on UGC CARE list year wise during the last five years" [3 p43]; conference papers sit in 3.3.2, mixed with book chapters [3 p44]. The SSR never publishes a single "total publications" figure a database could be matched against.
- **CHE's "111" is a mixed count by design.** The line is "Research papers (SCI/SCOPUS/ABDC etc.) 111" [8 p23]. "etc." is an undefined set and no year window is printed, so it cannot be reproduced from the document alone. CHE also runs its own "International Journal of Multidisciplinary Research" (volumes 1-6, 2016-2025) — a self-published, non-indexed outlet [13], which no database count will ever show.
- **NIRF submissions carry no self-reported paper count at all.** The forms ask for intake, faculty, expenditure, IPR, sponsored research, consultancy and patents; publication metrics are computed by NIRF from Scopus and Web of Science, not typed in by the college [4a][10 p18]. That is why the PSIT NIRF PDFs show patents and grants but no paper count.
- **Patent figures count applications.** The register separates "Published" (application laid open) from "Granted" [2c]; the SSR narrative collapses them: "60 patents published and applied for put together" [3 p42].

## 4. Institute claims vs the 793 records in `papers.csv`

`numbers.json` reports 793 records for 2007-2026: 599 journal articles, 132 proceedings papers, 44 book chapters, 15 preprints, 2 "other"; 2026 is partial.

**Entity boundary.** 784 of 793 records mention PSIT or "Pranveer" in an affiliation string, **392 mention "Pharmacy"**, 59 mention "Higher Education" [12]. The file mixes the engineering college, the pharmacy college and CHE; the SSR and NIRF numbers describe the engineering college only. Nine records contain no PSIT string and entered by author-name matching — possible false positives, not institute output.

**Counting unit.** NAAC 3.3.1 counts only UGC-CARE journals. The database counts any DOI-bearing item: Scopus journals never on the CARE list, conference proceedings, book chapters. Overlapping sets, neither containing the other.

**Year windows.** The SSR covers academic years 2018-19 to 2022-23; the database uses calendar years: 2018: 8, 2019: 24, 2020: 27, 2021: 35, 2022: 50, 2023: 74, 2024: 157, 2025: 148, 2026: 234 (partial). 2018-2022 sums to 144 records against the SSR's verified 117 journal papers plus 105 books/conference items. For 2022-23 the SSR claims 37 UGC-CARE papers while the database holds 74 items dated 2023 across all three units. Different denominators, both defensible.

**The institute's own figures were cut during verification, and it published the cut.** The SSR annexure prints each metric twice, "before DVV Verification" and "after". For 3.3.1 the claimed series 148, 136, 87, 72, 147 became 37, 21, 18, 17, 24 [3 p89]; for 3.3.2, 50, 54, 27, 25, 26 became 36, 23, 21, 15, 10 [3 p90]; for grants, 690.44 lakh became 10.44 lakh in 2022-23 [3 p88-89]. This is the most useful fact on this page: the gap between a headline number and a checked number is normal, documented, and here published by the college itself.

**What I can and cannot check.** I verified that all 74 records dated 2023 and all 157 dated 2024 carry DOIs. I cannot check accepted-but-unpublished papers, whether a journal was on the UGC-CARE list in the year concerned, or how many of the 392 pharmacy-affiliated records the engineering college would claim. No 2024-25 or 2025-26 publication count appears in any public PSIT document I found, so the 2024-2026 rise has no institute counterpart — UNCERTAIN, and not evidence of anything.

## 5. Patents and funded projects

**PSIT patents.** The "Patent Published" tab is backed by a register of 52 applications (filed 2019-2023, assignee "Pranveer Singh Institute of Technology, Kanpur"), each with application number, inventor, title and a Drive link to a proof PDF: 37 published, 12 granted, 3 blank [2c]. A 6-page scanned summary also exists whose pages 2-6 have no text layer [2d].

**PSIT-CHE patents.** Seven applications itemised with numbers and dates, all 2024 [8 p21-22]; NIRF reports 5 published and 1 granted for 2024 [7 p2]. The two documents disagree by two, probably a filing-versus-publication distinction that neither states.

**Funded projects, PSIT.** Only aggregates are public: 3 projects and INR 4.42 crore in 2024-25 [4a p3]; innovation grants including DST NIDHI iTBI INR 4.03 crore [4b p3]; SSR narrative naming "a project of Rs. 5.85 Crore from DST UP, a project of Rs.1.15 crore from MSME and a project of Rs.12.5 lakhs from CST" [3 p42]. No list with PI names and sanction letters is public. The file titled "3.2.1 - List of Projects (2022-23)" is actually a list of student startups and student innovative projects [2e].

**Funded projects, PSIT-CHE.** Three named projects (ICSSR INR 5 lakh; two CSJM projects at INR 50,000) [8 p22], against NIRF's zero sponsored projects for 2022-23 to 2024-25 [7 p3]. Both can be true if the grants fell outside those financial years or were held by individuals rather than the college; the disclosure gives no dates. UNCERTAIN.

**Inconsistencies worth knowing.** The same institute reports patents published as 27/10/8 in Engineering but 27/4/4 in Overall and Innovation for the same calendar years [4a][4b][5]. The register lists 12 granted patents while NIRF 2026 reports 50 granted in 2024 alone [2c][4a]. I cannot reconcile these publicly; treat the 50 as UNCERTAIN.

## 6. Why NIRF and NAAC counts exist, and why they differ from a bibliometric database

NIRF is a scoring exercise. Its 2025 report states that "publications, citations and HCP pertaining to research output of applicant institutions were retrieved from Scopus (Elsevier Science) and Web of Science (Clarivate Analytics)", and that "data on patents published and granted was taken from Derwent Innovation" [10 p18]. Publications feed the "Research and Professional Practice" head through "Combined metric for Publications (PU)" and "Combined metric for Quality of Publications (QP)" [11]. A college cannot inflate a NIRF paper count by typing a bigger number; it loses points if its faculty's papers are not indexed.

NAAC is an audit. Criterion III ("Research, Innovations and Extension") is answered with lists and proofs, then checked by DVV officers who accept or reject items — hence 148 becoming 37. The surviving number is what a peer team can defend, not what a brochure says.

A bibliometric database answers a third question: what exists in the scholarly record with a persistent identifier. It includes pharmacy papers, chapters and preprints; it excludes items without a DOI and anything in press. No two of the three instruments measure the same thing, and a factor-of-four gap between a brochure figure and a database count is normal — not proof of misconduct.

## How confident is this?

- **Solid:** every figure in the table was read out of the PDF page named, from the file saved under `01_psit_data/raw/institute_docs/`. The SSR's before/after DVV tables (p87-95) are the institute's own publication of both numbers, so the 148→37 example needs no interpretation.
- **Solid:** the entity split. NIRF IDs IR-E-C-46844 and IR-C-C-54316 are different institutions with different NAAC status, and the pharmacy unit appears only in affiliation strings.
- **UNCERTAIN:** the "50 patents granted (2024)" in NIRF'2026 contradicts both the 12-granted register and the 5/7/3 pattern at CHE. I could not find a list of 50 grants; do not quote it without checking.
- **UNCERTAIN:** which NIRF cycle the SSR's "Rank 151-300" refers to, and whether CHE's ICSSR project falls inside the three financial years NIRF reports as zero.
- **Not covered:** AQARs after 2024 and any 2024-25/2025-26 publication count — not published anywhere public that I could find; the R&D cell would have to supply them.
